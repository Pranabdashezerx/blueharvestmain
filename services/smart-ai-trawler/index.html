Once a vessel leaves the harbour, the owner often loses sight of the trip. The landing, the grade, and the price then sit in a separate set of books.

BlueHarvest joins those two sides. The hardware records the voyage. The market record picks up the same catch after it is landed.

From the first voyage position to the final seafood transaction, we aim to make every stage more connected, measurable, and accountable.

01

The voyage

Every fishing journey generates valuable information

A trip is more than the harbour, the ground, and the return. Weather, water, and the work on deck all belong in the same record.

From departure to landing

  1. Before departure

    Weather, ocean information, and earlier trips can be reviewed before the vessel leaves.

  2. During the voyage

    The system records where the vessel goes. Fitted sensors can add air temperature, humidity, and wind.

  3. During fishing activities

    Fishing events are stored with a place and a time, so a later trip can be compared with the conditions and the catch.

  4. On return

    On landing, the voyage can be tied to species, quantity, grade, and the sale.

02

The hardware

A connected platform built around the vessel

A vessel-mounted hardware platform collects the trip, organises it, and keeps it ready to review.

What the system can capture

Location and movement

Satellite coordinates build the track of the trip and tie every other reading to the same voyage.

Weather and atmosphere

Where sensors are fitted, the record includes air temperature, humidity, and wind, read alongside the forecast.

Marine and ocean conditions

Water temperature, currents, sea-surface conditions, and depth may come from the vessel or from satellite and forecast sources. A reading taken on board stays separate from outside data.

Fishing activity and events

Departure, movement, fishing, and the return are stored with a time and a position.

Camera and visual information

A camera can add context on deck or at landing. Computer vision is a separate workstream and depends on the final hardware and on software checks.

Edge controller and sync

The edge controller timestamps each reading, holds it on the vessel when coverage drops, and sends it when a connection returns.

IoT edge controller

A marine enclosure that collects sensor, GPS, and camera readings, stores them on board, and sends them when a connection is available.

Device status and monitoring

Lights for power, connectivity, and faults, so the crew can see whether the equipment is working.

GPS / GNSS antenna

Position, speed, course, and the route of the trip.

Temperature and humidity sensor

Air temperature and humidity around the vessel during the trip.

Wind speed sensor

Wind speed, and wind direction where a compatible sensor is fitted.

Connectors and cable harness

Sealed cables between the controller, the sensors, the antennas, and the vessel’s power.

Marine-grade mounting brackets

Corrosion-resistant mounts for antennas, weather sensors, and cameras.

Navigation and monitoring display

An onboard screen for position, the route, readings, and alerts.

Marine CCTV camera

A marine camera on the deck, the net area, or the landing point. The footage may later support computer vision.

Depth transducer

Water depth under the hull, stored with the vessel’s position.

Fuel consumption monitoring

Diesel used on the trip, from a compatible flow sensor or engine interface.

Fish-hold temperature sensor

Temperature in the hold, with an alert when a reading leaves the set range.

Sonar and fish-finder integration

A link to compatible sonar or fish-finder equipment, stored with position and conditions where the instrument allows it.

Digital catch logging

Authorised crew record species, quantity, weight, place, and time. Those notes can later meet the landing and the sale.

Engine RPM and diagnostics

RPM, hours, and available diagnostics from a compatible engine interface, read next to fuel use and the route.

Atmospheric conditions, wind, water depth, and integrated sonar information.

Operational efficiency

Fuel consumption, engine performance, and fishing activity monitoring.

Catch quality and traceability

Data-driven decision support

03

Connected intelligence

Sea, satellite, and market, studied together

A position says where the vessel went. A weather reading says what the air was doing. A catch note says what came ashore. The useful picture is those three, read together.

Onboard vessel information

Coordinates, movement, time, sensor readings, and fishing events: what happened on the trip, and where.

Satellite and ocean intelligence

Sea-surface temperature, ocean colour, water movement, and weather, matched to the vessel’s place and time. This is context. It does not mean fish will be there.

Historical fishing information

About five years of fishing and commercial records, once they have been checked: places, species, quantities, and season. A pattern has to be tested before it can guide a recommendation.

Seafood demand and market information

Demand, incoming supply, grade, storage, and price decide what the trip is worth after the fish is ashore.

04

Decision support

Better information for the people operating the vessel

The sea, the rules, fuel, and the crew still decide the trip. The system informs that judgement. It does not replace it.

Fishing-ground selection

Past grounds, conditions, and marine data are being studied for advisory notes on where to fish. The licensed operator decides, within the rules and the weather.

Voyage and route planning

The voyage record can show extra miles and time spent searching. Any saving on fuel has to be shown on the vessel itself.

Visibility for vessel owners

An owner can see where the vessel went, how long each activity took, and how this trip compares with earlier ones.

Responsible fishing

The record can show designated and restricted areas, and it can support later notes on bycatch. It does not replace statutory monitoring.

05

The record

Every trip should tell a complete story

The trip at sea and the sale ashore should be one story.

Trip notes, the landing book, the grade, and the sale often live apart. BlueHarvest ties them to one voyage, from departure through storage and dispatch.

What a connected record can help answer

  • Where did the vessel operate?
  • When did the fishing activity take place?
  • What environmental conditions were recorded or referenced?
  • Which species and quantities were landed?
  • How was the seafood graded?
  • What price did the catch achieve?
  • Where was the seafood stored or dispatched?
06

From catch to commerce

Connecting fishing operations with the marketplace

After the vessel returns, the catch is graded, traded, stored, and dispatched.

  1. Landing and catch identification

    Species, weight, quantity, and the source vessel are written against the trip as the catch comes ashore.

  2. Quality assessment and grading

    Grading uses inspection and a set procedure. Computer vision for species, size, and freshness is a separate workstream, and it needs training and a shared standard. Formalin, heavy metals, and other chemical checks need a validated test. A photograph cannot establish them.

  3. Digital trading and price discovery

    After grading, the lot can be shown to buyers, including buyers who are not on the floor.

  4. Storage and fulfilment

    Cold storage can hold fish that is not sold at once. That storage stays tied to the sale and the dispatch.

07

The intelligence layer

From recorded journeys to informed recommendations

Each trip, landing, and sale adds a result. Models built on those records are still being developed.

Fishing-location intelligence

Place, season, conditions, and past catch, as advisory guidance on where to look. Not a promise of fish.

Operational efficiency

Trip length, route, and result, compared once enough voyages have been checked.

Species and season

Which species show up, by season and by stretch of coast.

Market and price

Supply, demand, grade, and price, toward later guidance on when and where to sell.
